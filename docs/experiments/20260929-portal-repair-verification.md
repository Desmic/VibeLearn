The scoped repair caches the selected orbit for an exact player position, yaw, pitch, zoom and static-camera-geometry revision. It still rechecks clearance from the smoothed camera pose every update. Geometry refreshes bounds and invalidates the result when the camera-solid list length changes; future same-length list replacements or in-place moving/reshaping solids must explicitly call `invalidateCameraGeometry()`. The cache does not claim to accelerate dynamic obstacles.

One owned Chrome process compared cache off/on/on/off sequentially against the **same** clean-portal bundle and then closed. Six fixed player/orbit probes matched camera position and movement bearing within `1e-6` with cache off and on. Explicit geometry invalidation changed repeated-probe ray-query counts from 4 to 2 and back to 4. Held-key approach, orbit, pause (no frame advance while paused), and restart passed. At the sampled static wall poses, cache-on used one ray per update and measured 2.04–2.35 ms camera JavaScript per update; cache-off used five to eleven rays and measured 10.48–23.56 ms. These are instrumented main-thread camera times, not GPU or whole-frame costs. Timed held movement reached slightly different wall positions across runs, so the 41.6–48.5 ms short frame-interval medians cannot establish a causal frame-rate gain. Near-wall play remains slower than the desired smooth cadence. The same build produced 146 draw calls and 167,312 triangles at arrival with either cache setting; the camera change adds no rendered geometry. The browser loaded the clean model and manifest with HTTP 200 and recorded no page or resource failures.
