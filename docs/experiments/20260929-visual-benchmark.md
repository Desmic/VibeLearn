## Result: rejected as the next game art candidate

The worker's two visual passes are complete. Larger depth, real environment
lighting and a textured ground did not establish the reference's distinctive
graphic techno-fantasy world. The second pass made warm surfaces excessively
brown. Repeated slabs, cones and rings still read as construction primitives;
the oversized tree/planet do not establish a credible inhabited destination.
Water lacks depth and convincing edge treatment. The walk is still a corridor
with distant scenery rather than a compelling invitation to explore. Distinctive
companions and foreground/world proportions remain inadequate. These are visual
production failures, not proof of a PlayCanvas limitation.

Native worker play observed both signal responses, reset clearing the response,
camera orbit, keyboard input, phone-width garden activation and the pause toggle.
This is limited lab evidence: a few key presses do not certify continuous
traversal/collision quality. The lab has no complete opening sequence and cannot
pass its story/learning gates. Final screenshots are
`artifacts/visual-benchmark-20260929/desktop-final.png` and `phone-final.png`.
The phone view is functional but crowded; these are failure evidence, not
acceptance pictures. No new cold critic score was requested for a candidate the
worker already rejects.

The implementation reused the pinned PlayCanvas renderer, robot, companions,
foliage and controls. Architecture uses combined static meshes by material and
shadow policy. Official Poly Haven CC0 HDR and surface assets total 3,423,685
source bytes; exact URLs, author attribution and checksums are in
`web/lab/assets/PROVENANCE.json`. Their source size is not decoded GPU memory.
No package installation, active gameplay route, learner data or deployment was
changed. A redundant lab animation loop/statistics poll was removed; UI anchor
updates follow engine rendered frames, including demand-rendered pause updates.
This local cleanup does not establish a whole-game CPU/GPU improvement.

## What changes in production now

1. **Approve visible form before adding systems.** Start with one representative
   playable view, then turn/move through it. Evaluate architecture silhouettes,
   landmark, ground/water edges, inhabitants and negative space against the
   reference. A convincing still alone is insufficient; an unconvincing still
   is an early stop signal. Do not repeat reference research without a specific
   unresolved production question.
2. **Use a coherent small asset kit.** Inventory existing usable assets before
   shopping or coding. Select compatible architecture, foliage and character
   treatment with known licenses, scale, pivots, material response and cost.
   Author only the identity-bearing gaps: a distinctive branching canopy,
   continuous curved city terraces and readable inhabited destinations. Adding
   an HDR or realistic texture to generic primitives is not this step. A kit
   must be visible in the first prototype, not promised for a later polish pass.
3. **Give Sol a complete bounded deliverable.** Asset preparation, mesh/material
   organization and focused measurements can run independently. Root retains
   visual direction and the early camera-level verdict. No parallel people
   changing the same scene or repeated status-only supervision.
4. **Separate exploration cost from acceptance cost.** Asset loading, syntax,
   actual control/interaction and short performance checks during the trial;
   full application/browser/presentation tests after a worthwhile integration.
   One fresh Astra then supplies cold observations and all critic lanes. Tests
   stay mandatory at the integrated gate; weak art does not get certified by
   more tests or more reviewers.
5. **Keep the system boundary earned.** Promote reusable asset/palette/lighting,
   placement and performance-budget needs only after the sample demonstrates
   their value. Preserve package-specific art choices outside shared controllers.
   Do not turn the disposable procedural geometry builder here into the platform.

The next decision is an asset/composition proof, not automatic engine migration.
An isolated Three.js comparison is justified if the same content/lighting task
exposes a concrete PlayCanvas integration or authoring obstacle. Match assets,
viewport, resolution, shadows and camera before comparing speed/cost. Changing
engines while preserving these weak forms would not test the actual bottleneck.
The prior active v18 candidate remains the last integrated checkpoint, with its
existing limitations; neither it nor this lab is reference-quality accepted.

new asset bytes. Both desktop 1280x720 and phone-emulated 390x844 loaded assets
without page/request errors, produced the distinct route replies and cleared

from the active game. Semantic entity counts omit lab-native architecture.

slow rAF pacing (desktop median 783 ms, phone-emulated 300 ms). Hardware
acceleration identity was not established; neither 60 fps nor 30 fps is verified.
The engine triangle counter returned zero despite active draws and is invalid
for reporting. CPU/GPU utilization was not measured. Do not dismiss slow pacing
as proven software rendering or claim physical-phone performance from emulation.

this unintegrated, art-rejected lab. Owned native/test browser tabs and local
8055 server were closed/stopped.
