# Ten-game study: worlds that invite meaningful play

Research snapshot: 20 September 2026. This expands `LIVING-WORLD-DESIGN.md`.
Purpose: improve the general authoring and criticism system, with the current
entry-to-Level-1 journey as a bounded proof. These are design hypotheses, not a
claim to have identified the causes of commercial success.

## Selection and evidence

Metacritic helps shortlist; it does not measure learning, accessibility, artistic
fit or production feasibility. Scores below are platform-specific critic scores
retrieved on this date, not averages across ports. Cached pages may lag changes.
The order is descending score, with no significance attached to ties. This is a
deliberately varied selection, not the ten highest-scoring games ever made.

Award coverage includes [BG3, TGA 2023](https://thegameawards.com/rewind/year-2023),
[Elden Ring, TGA 2022](https://thegameawards.com/rewind/year-2022),
[Hades, BAFTA Best Game 2021](https://www.bafta.org/awards/games/?award-year=2021)
and [Outer Wilds, BAFTA Best Game 2020](https://www.bafta.org/media-centre/press-releases/winners-announced-british-academy-games-awards-2020/).
Outer Wilds is intentionally retained despite its lower aggregate score because
its knowledge-driven design addresses our purpose directly. Rockstar is represented
by RDR2; a second Rockstar game would currently contribute less variety.

**Evidence boundary:** no reference game was played in this study. Gameplay
narration, developer interviews and commentary are evidence of demonstrated
features or stated intent, not independent proof of the entire shipped experience.
Prerelease demonstrations are labeled. Auto-captions can misrecognize names;
timestamps locate passages, not exact quotations. No audio or motion-quality
certification is claimed. Recommendations below are our interpretations.

## 1. Ocarina of Time: learning changes the meaning of space

**Evidence:** the full official [new remake demonstration transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PQvD3p2yGwc)
and selected frames were examined in the [dedicated study](experiments/20260920-ocarina-gameplay-study.md).
The 99 score above belongs to the 1998 game; it must never be assigned to the
unreleased remake. The new demonstration is a separate, prerelease evidence source.

**Concrete mechanisms.** At 6:58 a fall and climbable vines show recovery within
the environment. The slingshot acquired at 7:30 serves combat, then a bridge/eye
interaction around 8:04–8:26. The same capability is useful in different contexts.
At 9:03–10:18 the field and distant castle support orientation, with optional
story/objective assistance. Town alleys, conversation and activities at
11:18–12:03 provide reasons to linger beyond reaching the next dungeon.

**Our interpretation.** A landmark becomes more valuable when it promises a
future action; teaching is stronger when a new action changes how the player
reads existing scenery. A village feels inhabited when it supports ordinary
life, not merely a row of mission dispensers. Recovery can preserve spatial
continuity and the player's willingness to experiment.

**Transfer and limit.** Author an initial safe use and a later, visibly related
but unprompted use of a capability. Do not copy the eye switch, village, characters
or exact sequence. Ability gates suit some games; they are not a universal
requirement. Optional hints must remain discoverable to someone genuinely stuck.

**Critic experiment:** after tutorial success, can the player recognize a new
application without following an identical marker? On a deliberate mistake, can
they recover and retain their bearings? Record actions and explanations separately.

## 2. Breath of the Wild: consistent rules create possibilities

**Evidence:** full [Nintendo Open-Air Concept documentary transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vLMGrmf4xaY),
2017. Available captions were Japanese auto-captions; this study uses our English
paraphrase, not an official English transcript. At 2:33–3:38 developers discuss
scale in relation to traversal and real-world distances. At 3:39–5:58 cooking and
physics-based combat illustrate experimentation; rolling a boulder downhill can
be a legitimate solution. At 9:21 onward they describe the team playing the
current build together to understand one another's work.

**Our interpretation.** Depth can come from interactions between a few reliable
rules. Expanding a map or adding verbs does not automatically increase agency.
The useful question is whether a player can predict an outcome, try it and receive
an intelligible response. Whole-journey play is also a production technique:
locally good components can make a poor combined experience.

**Transfer and limit.** For an educational simulation, let a rule used in one
problem operate consistently in another. Permit alternate valid approaches when
they demonstrate the required concept. Do not build a general physics sandbox
to imitate Zelda; combinations multiply implementation and verification costs.
An ingenious shortcut may finish the game without supplying learning evidence.

**Critic experiment:** propose a plausible alternate approach using already
taught rules, try it, and distinguish a supported solution from a bypass. Does
the journey remain understandable when played continuously from entry?

## 3. Red Dead Redemption 2: attachment through ordinary participation

**Evidence:** full [Rockstar official gameplay transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dw_oH5oiUSE),
2018 prerelease presentation, authored/unspecified English captions. At 2:13–2:51
camp is a base for eating, chores, games and stories; providing supplies affects
morale and spending time with people reveals opportunities. At 2:59–3:42 social
actions can escalate or defuse confrontations. At 4:14–4:39 horse treatment and
bonding connect a relationship to control in difficult situations.

**Our interpretation.** Repeated low-stakes participation can make a later threat
matter. Life is conveyed by activities with purposes and responses, not by NPC
count. A place can offer a social rhythm: departure, shared activity, changed
return. The emotional and practical roles of a companion can reinforce each other.

**Transfer and limit.** A small game can establish attachment through one shared
task, a recognizable routine and an acknowledgment on return. This is cheaper and
more legible than simulating a town. Avoid mandatory chores, long travel or
interaction friction that overwhelms a short learning session. A promotional
overview cannot establish the frequency or reliability of those responses.

**Critic experiment:** linger, approach someone before the objective, then return
after an action. Identify what changed and why. Can the player name a relationship
from experienced behavior rather than only a caption explaining it?

## 4. Baldur's Gate 3: identity must have consequences

**Evidence:** Larian's Emily Gera explains [character creation and world reactivity](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/09/14/how-your-choices-in-baldurs-gate-3s-character-creator-carry-through-your-entire-playthrough/),
September 2023, read through its substantive sections. Ancestry, class and
background can change responses and available insights. Background goals connect
roleplaying to situations, such as an entertainer seeking an audience. This is
a developer account, not a fresh gameplay audit of branching coverage.

**Our interpretation.** Personalization is meaningful when the world recognizes
it, not simply when the player selects a different appearance. Expression can
include choosing an approach, a social stance or a motivation. The resulting
acknowledgment needs to be observable, otherwise a real internal branch still
feels cosmetic to the player.

**Transfer and limit.** Offer a small number of supported approaches that preserve
the same learning outcome and make the chosen approach visibly matter. The system
should state what is genuinely personalized and what is shared. Do not reproduce
BG3's combinatorial scope or use inferred learner identity to stereotype behavior.
Dialogue branches alone are not evidence of conceptual understanding.

**Critic experiment:** compare two supported approaches and trace their actual
response differences. Then verify the learning requirement and assistance history
remain intact across both. Flag a choice whose promised consequence never appears.

## 5. Elden Ring: guidance that permits refusal

**Evidence:** full English auto-caption transcript of [Bandai Namco's gameplay preview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tw-zJ3bAg08),
2021 prerelease presentation. Grace points onward but exploration can diverge
(0:39–0:57); player markers and world-visible beacons support orientation
(3:49–4:26). Alexander's request makes a local encounter intelligible
(2:19–3:25). Stormveil offers a warned-against main gate and another entrance
(12:21–13:38); terrain and visibility invite different strategies (14:20–15:25).

**Our interpretation.** Mystery and guidance are compatible. A world can indicate
an important direction while allowing the player to postpone it. Alternate routes
matter when their risk and character differ, not merely their geometry. A small,
understandable encounter can interrupt a large intimidating world with personality.

**Transfer and limit.** Distinguish the required learning destination from optional
exploration, and provide a route back. Let world signals express different
approaches. Do not equate obscurity or punishing combat with quality; a learner
who is lost in the interface cannot productively engage with conceptual difficulty.
Short sessions need stronger recovery and orientation than this reference assumes.

**Critic experiment:** intentionally ignore guidance, explore, and return. Can the
player recover direction without a reset? At a choice, can they explain the visible
tradeoff before committing, without needing to predict every consequence?

## 6. Portal 2: design for understanding, not passive success

**Evidence:** [Valve in-game commentary, recorded by a third-party uploader](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HE7o8RMoQPw),
English auto-captions; the opening through 8:28 was read, not the entire recording.
At 1:41–2:34 the team explains replacing a difficult-to-explain simulated hotel
opening with a physical container reveal. At 5:41–6:09 a portal lesson changed
from timed movement to player-selected destinations because players could simply
wait for the right configuration. At 7:35–8:28 the commentary links satisfying
discovery to iterative puzzle ordering and cheaply revisable connections.

**Our interpretation.** A tutorial must cause the player to make the relevant
decision. Progress alone can conceal non-learning. Spatial staging can explain
a situation faster than exposition, while modular experience boundaries make
large teaching revisions affordable.

**Transfer and limit.** Identify what decision demonstrates the target idea, then
remove accidental routes to apparent success in the assessment step. Keep
experimentation safe and preserve legitimate alternate solutions. Reorder coherent
chunks when the learning sequence fails; do not protect sunk work. An overly
constrained task can still become guess-the-designer's-answer.

**Critic experiment:** attempt to succeed by waiting, following highlights or
repeating a fixed action. If that works, ask whether the evidence contract still
correctly marks understanding as unknown. Follow with an unprompted transfer case.

## 7. Hades: a failed attempt can still move the relationship forward

**Evidence:** Greg Kasavin's [Origin Story interview transcript](https://www.originstory.show/episodes/greg-kasavin),
especially 8:27–20:57 and 23:13–26:59. He describes early access feedback, a low
barrier to starting play, and choosing a roguelike structure before shaping its
narrative. Around 17:22–20:57 he explains aligning the protagonist's and bosses'
memory with the player's repeated attempts, making death part of continuing story.

**Our interpretation.** Retrying need not erase emotional progress. A response
that recognizes what happened can make another attempt feel like continuation.
The story works with the repeated activity rather than pretending repetition
never occurred. Immediate accessibility and later depth can coexist.

**Transfer and limit.** Keep the learner's attempt, acknowledge the specific error,
offer an appropriate next step, and let the world remember a bounded relevant
event. This is not permission to award mastery for persistence or silently lower
the standard. Repeated novelty and branching dialogue are expensive; a concise,
truthful acknowledgment can be enough. Do not require failure to see essential
story or turn practice into compulsory grinding.

**Critic experiment:** make two different mistakes. Does recovery distinguish them,
preserve evidence and avoid replaying irrelevant exposition? Does the second
attempt offer a reason to continue while still requiring the intended skill?

## 8. The Witcher 3: places and tasks need human reasons

**Evidence:** [Patrick Mills interview](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-witcher-3-wild-hunt/the-witcher-quest-design-cd-projekt-masterclass),
2016, read in full. Mills connects quests to motivations and complete narrative
arcs, aligns character desire with player desire, and discusses uncertain or
delayed consequences. In the [Monnier/Sasko interview](https://www.gamesradar.com/witcher-3s-world-building-and-quest-design-explained-cd-projekt-red-2/),
settlement placement begins with geography and plausible needs; encounter density
was repeatedly adjusted through play. These are firsthand developer statements
published by press outlets. The official 35-minute gameplay upload and a mirror
returned no transcript; neither is counted as a completed transcript study.

**Additional process evidence:** [CDPR's 2026 level-design transcript](https://www.cdprojektred.com/en/blog/188/answered-podcast-episode-30-paths-and-possibilities-the-art-of-level-design-transcript-included),
sections on team origins, agency and guidance, describes Witcher 3's evolving
spatial-planning practice. Its later examples also concern Cyberpunk and future
projects; those must not be relabeled as Witcher 3 features.

**Our interpretation.** A settlement's work, resources and routes can explain its
layout. A task is more memorable when an outcome matters to someone, and the
player has a reason to care before accepting it. Density is an experience to tune,
not a fixed number of props or encounters per square meter.

**Transfer and limit.** Give each important place a purpose, need and visible trace
of use. Give each mission a person affected, an actionable problem and a consequence.
Use moral ambiguity where appropriate to the audience; do not make foundational
learning feedback ambiguous. Delayed story consequences can coexist with immediate
feedback about what an action did. Avoid assuming every short activity needs a
large branching subplot.

**Critic experiment:** before reading the objective, infer what the place is for;
after the mission, explain who benefited or what changed. Test whether the route
contains meaningful pauses or merely empty travel and repetitive interruptions.

## 9. Expedition 33: a premise expressed through art and mechanics

**Evidence:** [Xbox's developer interview transcript](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/podcast/deep-dive-into-expedition-33-official-xbox-podcast/),
read through the art, traversal and character-system discussion. Broche describes
a Paintress premise shaping the cast's relationship to a world of shrinking
lifespans. Maxson-Francombe describes location identity through color and shape,
including red grass against white sculptural heads. Character-specific mechanics
such as Lune's elemental Stains and Sciel's Foretell support different approaches.
These were prerelease statements of intent.

Two frames sampled from [Kepler's early gameplay demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YqOYp-bWtHg)
showed a conversation against a cool blue environment and a battle target with
an ornate pale-green body, dark red mane and red vegetation behind it. Those are
visual observations only; no full-video, traversal-quality or combat-feel claim.
The transcript exporter returned no transcript for that upload.

**Our interpretation.** Art direction needs a recognizable set of choices, not
just the adjective beautiful. A premise can shape what people fear, how places
look and what individual actions mean. Distinction between characters can be
expressed through decisions as well as appearance.

**Transfer and limit.** Define a few original shape, palette, material and cultural
motifs, and explain their relationship to the premise. Give each location one
legible identity within that shared language. Do not reproduce Belle Epoque assets,
the Paintress or character systems. Decorative contrast does not guarantee that
interactive targets or faces remain readable on a phone. Constant emotional
intensity can also leave too little room for attachment and reflection.

**Critic experiment:** describe the location's identity without its name or lore;
then inspect whether shapes and contrast support actual choices at gameplay
distance. Separate attractive staging from usable staging and from understood stakes.

## 10. Outer Wilds: curiosity has an information structure

**Evidence:** [Kelsey Beachum's GDC talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QaGu9tGCNbI),
English auto-captions, studied through 22:12. At 3:34–7:48 questions lead to
exploration and useful knowledge; learning that a cave fills with sand changes
future navigation. At 13:19–17:52 information is available for the player to seek
and revisit. At 17:53–19:36 dialogue can offer a gentle starting destination when
the player expresses uncertainty. This corrects the simplistic idea that
curiosity-driven design means leaving everybody without guidance.

Alex Beachum's [prototype retrospective](https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/outer-wilds-text-adventure.html),
read in full, describes testing the overarching mystery in a paper/text prototype
before realizing it in-engine. This supports a cheap coherence check, not a
replacement for embodied playtesting.

**Our interpretation.** Information becomes valuable when it changes what the
player can infer or do. A mystery needs linked questions and useful partial answers,
not merely withheld exposition. Optional assistance can preserve agency while
reducing avoidable confusion.

**Transfer and limit.** Design question → investigation → evidence → revised
prediction → application. Let observations remain revisitable. Test that the
information graph is understandable before expensive world production, then test
its spatial delivery through GUI play. A known password or memorized answer can
bypass discovery; completion alone still cannot establish learner understanding.
Some learners need explicit goals sooner than this reference audience does.

**Critic experiment:** ask what the player currently wonders, what clue changed
their belief, and what they can now attempt. If they cannot identify a question,
more hidden lore is unlikely to fix the opening.

## Synthesis: choose a coherent approach, not all ten at once

The following are our design decisions inferred from the evidence, not universal
rules established by these games' scores.

| Tension | Appropriate choice for our system |
| --- | --- |
| Ocarina's staged capability learning vs BOTW's experimentation | Choose how much grammar must be learned first; preserve consistent rules afterward. |
| Elden Ring's optional direction vs Portal's controlled lesson | Permit uncertainty about discovery; remove uncertainty about controls and required evidence. |
| RDR2's ordinary shared life vs Hades' repeated short attempts | Match relationship-building to session length and retry frequency. |
| BG3's expressive branching vs Expedition's focused premise | Budget a few meaningful choices or a strong authored journey; neither is automatically superior. |
| Witcher's uncertain consequences vs learning feedback | Preserve narrative complexity while making causal task feedback useful and assessable. |
| Outer Wilds' knowledge progress vs rewards and upgrades | Make understanding useful in play; keep reward state separate from mastery evidence. |

**Working definition of a living world:** a place with intelligible purpose,
recognizable identity, reasons to care, opportunities to act, and responses that
the player can perceive. This can be one room. NPC schedules, huge maps and
photorealism are possible techniques, not mandatory ingredients.

## How this changes the general system's work

Use the existing authoring brief and critic lanes, not ten new critic agents or
another numeric score. Each chosen technique becomes a small decision record:
source and locator; intended player experience; concrete implementation;
failure case; production cost; observable verification; and explicit exclusions.
At least one decision must address art/world direction. Source prestige never
counts as evidence that our implementation works.

| Design obligation | Existing review owner | Evidence that would challenge it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A reason to care before an instruction to act | Story + art/world | Cold player can repeat captions but cannot identify a relationship or stake. |
| Consistent action and clue language | Gameplay + art/world | Plausible action fails silently; important clue reads like decoration. |
| A decision that uses the learning concept | Learning + gameplay | Waiting, guessing or a fixed sequence passes the supposed mastery check. |
| Legible identity and usable space | Art/world's six existing dimensions | A good still image becomes occluded, confusing or crowded during normal play. |
| Recovery that preserves progress honestly | Gameplay + learning + story | Retry loses evidence, implies false mastery or forgets what just happened. |
| Meaningful visible consequence | Story + art/world + gameplay | State changes internally but the player cannot perceive its effect. |

These are authoring/review applications, not new automated enforcement implemented
by this research change. Existing validators enforce evidence structure; a fresh
Astra reviewer must judge the actual experience. Keep research and intent away
from the cold-observer pass, then use them in the intent-comparison pass.

## Bounded application after this research

1. **Opening:** repair known caption-dependent relationships and phone focal
   hierarchy. Specify one visible shared activity or dependency and one legible
   invitation. Confirm cold understanding before extending the tutorial.
2. **Tutorial into Level 1:** define safe initial use and a genuinely different
   transfer use; check that markers cannot substitute for understanding. Keep the
   separate tutorial and its clean success.
3. **Level 1 and recovery:** make route clues jointly readable, preserve camera
   clearance, and make consequences/retry acknowledgment perceivable. Prefer fewer
   purposeful elements to more decoration.

These are ordered hypotheses to test against the existing native findings, not
completed game improvements. Motion/audio review remain deferred. No readiness
upgrade, Level 2, deployment or live Terminal PM run follows from this study.
The future separate research stage is specified in `GAME-RESEARCH-PIPELINE.md`.
